Hands-free reading scroll

A button at the right of the toolbar sets the page moving at a steady reading pace. While it runs, the button opens into a small control: play/pause inside a ring that fills as you go down the page, a speed slider with the time left beside it, and stop. It all stays in the toolbar and never covers or moves the page. The stage below is live, so try it.

What stops or pauses it

Your own scrolling never stops it. The wheel, the trackpad, the scrollbar and the arrow, Page Up/Down, Home and End keys all move the page as usual, so you can flick back to reread or jump ahead. Reading holds while you scroll and carries on from where you let go, about half a second later. Scrolling back up from the end starts it reading again.

Typing in a field or dragging to select text pauses it. Pressing Space or play carries on.

The bottom of the page pauses it and says so. On pages that load more as you go, like Reddit or X, it waits for the new posts and keeps going.

Leaving the page, through a link or back, stops it. Switching tabs pauses it, and each tab keeps its own state.

The agent acting in the tab (a click or a page change) pauses it, so you and the agent don't fight over where the page is.

Speed

Drag the slider anywhere from ¼× to 8×. It runs on a log scale, so the slow end is as easy to set as the fast end. It clicks onto ½×, 1×, 2× and 4× as you pass them, which the dots mark.

Scroll over the control to nudge it, and double-click the speed to go back to 1×.

1× is about 30 pixels a second, an unhurried read of an article column. 8× is about 240, fast enough to skim a feed. Under the speed is how long the rest of the page takes at that speed, and it updates as you drag.

The ring around play fills as you go down the page. The last speed you chose is used next time, in every tab.
